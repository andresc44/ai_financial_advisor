================================================================================
0. WHAT FINNBOT DOES
================================================================================

Finnbot sends you a stock-market email (the "digest") on trading days, and optionally Telegram
messages when something about your holdings needs attention.

The digest can include:
  - Markets and macro: graded market signals (trend, mood, breadth, rates, the dollar, oil) and
    the day's top macro news.
  - Swing ideas: US stocks set up for a move over the next few weeks, with an entry, stop, and
    target.
  - Long-term ideas: US businesses that look worth owning for years at today's price.
  - Your portfolio (if you connect holdings): its value, the day's change, unrealized gain or
    loss, which positions need attention, upcoming earnings, a review of risks across the whole
    portfolio (with a chart of how it's spread across sectors), and a card for each holding (its
    price, your average purchase price, gain or loss, signal, reasoning, and news).
  - A daily price chart for every US stock, on its card (holdings and ideas alike).

Each holding gets one of five signals from an AI model:
  Buy more     The stock has pulled back and the data suggests it will recover: a time to add.
  Hold         Healthy. Give it time.
  Risky        Something looks off and needs watching. Selling to be safe may make sense.
  Profit sell  It has risen, but the indicators say the run may turn down soon.
  Urgent sell  A specific, time-sensitive problem. The only signal that sends a Telegram alert.

These signals are AI-generated opinions based on market data and news. They are not financial
advice, and the same stock can occasionally get a different signal on two runs with the same data.
Always make your own decisions. Sections 8 and 9 explain how the ideas are found and how your
holdings are assessed.

Who pays for what:
  - The ideas, the market signals, and the macro news are shared by everyone. Andres covers them.
  - Anything about YOUR holdings (the AI review of each stock, its news, and the portfolio review)
    runs on YOUR OWN API keys, so those providers bill you directly:
      Anthropic (the reviews): about $1-2 a month for 10-20 holdings with both digests, plus a
        few cents to a dollar or so for the watchdog.
      Google (the news, optional): about $0.25-0.55 a month for 10-20 holdings with both digests.
        It needs billing turned on for your Gemini key: Google's free tier doesn't include the web
        search the news uses.
    Section 3.10 has the full table.
  - An ideas-only user (section 2.3) pays nothing.


================================================================================
1. THE BASICS (EVERYONE)
================================================================================

1.1 Your name
-------------
What it is: the name Finnbot greets you with ("Good morning, Steven"). It's also the label Andres
uses for your settings, so it must be different from every other user's.
Format: your first name, letters only (a hyphen is fine).

1.2 Your email address
----------------------
What it is: where the digest is sent.
Recommendation: a Gmail address, read in the Gmail app. The email is designed for a phone, and its
dropdowns and "jump to this stock" taps work in Gmail once you allow them (1.4). Other email apps
show a standard version of the same content.

1.3 Which digests you want
--------------------------
There are two digests each trading day (a day the US or Canadian stock market is open). Times are
US Eastern time (ET, New York's time). Each takes about 20-30 minutes to build, so it arrives a
little after it starts.

  Pre-Market digest   Starts at 7:00 ET, before the US market opens at 9:30 ET.
                      Covers the previous session's moves, and live moves on markets that are
                      already open (for example London).
  Post-Market digest  Starts at 4:10 PM ET, 10 minutes after the US market closes.
                      Covers the full day that just ended.

Your options:
  Both          Pros: the most up to date; morning plan and evening wrap-up.
                Cons: about twice the AI cost for your holdings (a bit less with news on, since
                the evening reuses the morning's news; section 3.10).
  Pre-Market    Pros: arrives before the market opens, so you can plan the day.
  only          Cons: US figures are from the previous day.
  Post-Market   Pros: the day's final results.
  only          Cons: arrives in the evening in North America, and overnight in Asia-Pacific
                (for example about 4:40 AM in Perth).
Ideas only (2.3) costs you nothing either way, so both is the natural choice there.

Converting ET to your time (approximate; daylight saving shifts these by an hour for part of the
year): Toronto and New York: same. Bogota: 1 hour earlier for most of the year. London: 5 hours
later. Perth: 12 hours later (13 in the northern winter). Sydney: 14-16 hours later.

1.4 Make the email's dropdowns work in Gmail (dynamic email)
-------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: the digest is mostly dropdowns (each section, each stock), so you can open only what
you want. Gmail shows these working dropdowns ("dynamic email") only for senders you have allowed.
Without this step, Gmail shows a plainer version with everything already open.
Who it's for: Gmail users. Other email apps always show the plainer version.
Steps (once, on a computer; the Gmail app has no such setting, but it follows your account's
choice):
  1. Open Gmail in a web browser (mail.google.com), signed in to the account the digest goes to.
  2. Click the gear icon at the top right, then "See all settings".
  3. On the General tab, scroll to "Dynamic email" and tick "Enable dynamic email".
  4. Click "Developer settings" next to it, add Finnbot's sending address:
       finnbot107@gmail.com
     and click OK.
  5. Scroll to the bottom of the General tab and click "Save Changes".
After this, the next digest opens with its dropdowns working, in the Gmail app too. Digests older
than 30 days go back to the plainer version (a Gmail rule).
Cost: none.

2. THE MAIN DECISION: HOW FINNBOT SEES YOUR HOLDINGS
================================================================================

Choose one of three options. This choice decides which of the later sections apply to you. In the
sign-up form they read:
  "Link automatically to brokerage, RECOMMENDED (through SnapTrade)"   = option A
  "Manually update a Google Sheet"                                     = option B
  "No holdings, just suggestions to buy (free)"                        = option C

  Option              Effort to set up   Stays up to date        Costs you
  ------------------  -----------------  ----------------------  -------------------------------
  A. SnapTrade        Medium             Automatically           AI costs (section 3.10)
     (recommended)
  B. Google Sheets    Low-medium         Only when you edit it   AI costs (section 3.10)
  C. Ideas only       None               (no holdings)           Nothing

You can switch later (section 5).

2.1 Option A: SnapTrade (link your brokerage; recommended)
----------------------------------------------------------
What it is: SnapTrade is a service that connects to your brokerage account (for example Questrade
or Wealthsimple) and lets Finnbot READ your positions: what you own, how many shares, in which
accounts, and what you paid. Finnbot never places trades.

Pros:
  - Automatic: your holdings are always current, including trades you just made.
  - Every account at the brokerage is included (TFSA, RRSP, margin, ...), with the names your
    broker uses.
  - Your average cost comes from your broker, so no typing and no typos.
Cons:
  - More steps to set up (below).
  - A third party (SnapTrade) gets read access to your brokerage account.
  - Brokerage connections occasionally expire and need you to log in again through SnapTrade.
    Finnbot tells you when this happens (it also tells you if an account hasn't synced in 4 days).
  - Not every brokerage is supported. Check SnapTrade's list of supported brokerages first.
  - If your broker doesn't report what you paid for a position, its gain or loss shows as unknown.

IMPORTANT, READ BEFORE CHOOSING SNAPTRADE:
  SnapTrade's own guidance says a Personal API key "must not be distributed in an app or shared
  with other people", and that it "can access your brokerage data and may be able to trade where
  trading is enabled". Finnbot only ever reads your positions, but sending the key to Andres means
  trusting him with that access. Choose read-only access when SnapTrade offers it (step 3), and if
  you'd rather not share that access at all, Google Sheets (option B) avoids the issue entirely,
  because nothing is connected to your brokerage.

Steps (from SnapTrade's documentation; the dashboard's exact wording may differ):
  1. Go to snaptrade.com and sign up for a free Personal account.
  2. Turn on two-factor authentication for your SnapTrade account (SnapTrade requires it for
     Personal API keys, and it protects your brokerage link).
  3. In the SnapTrade dashboard, connect your brokerage: choose your broker and log in to it in
     SnapTrade's secure connection window. If you're offered read-only access, choose it: Finnbot
     never needs to trade.
  4. Open the dashboard's API Key page and create your Personal API key. You get two values: a
     Client ID and a Consumer Key. The Consumer Key works like a password to your brokerage data:
     store both right away as described in section 6.
  5. Send both to Andres through the private channel he gives you (section 6), never through the
     sign-up form, email, or a chat app's group.
Cost: SnapTrade says Personal users are currently free. Finnbot adds no charge.

2.2 Option B: Google Sheets (type your holdings in a spreadsheet)
------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: you keep a Google Sheet listing your holdings, and Finnbot reads it at every digest
and watchdog check. Live prices, company names, and exchange rates come from Yahoo Finance
automatically; you only enter what you own and what you paid.

Pros:
  - Works with any brokerage, or several brokerages at once.
  - Nothing is connected to your brokerage account.
  - Full control over what Finnbot sees.
Cons:
  - Manual: after every buy or sell you must update the sheet, or Finnbot reviews old positions.
  - The layout must be exact (below), or rows are skipped or misread.
  - Only the exchanges listed below are fully supported.

Steps:
  1. Create a new Google Sheet (sheets.google.com, then Blank).
  2. On the FIRST tab (the one the spreadsheet was created with), type the column names below in
     row 1, exactly as written: lowercase, with underscores.
  3. Add one row per position, starting in row 2.
  4. Share the sheet with Finnbot only: click Share, paste Finnbot's service-account email address
     (finnbot-service@finnbot-504921.iam.gserviceaccount.com), set it to "Viewer", untick
     "Notify people", and click Share. Only you and Finnbot can see the sheet. (Don't use "Anyone
     with the link": then anyone who gets the link can see your holdings.)
  5. Send Andres the sheet's ID, PRIVATELY with your keys (section 6.3); the sign-up form doesn't
     ask for it. It's the long code in the sheet's web address, between "/d/" and "/edit". For
     example, in
       https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1AbCdEfGhIjKlMnOpQrStUvWxYz0123456789/edit#gid=0
     the ID is 1AbCdEfGhIjKlMnOpQrStUvWxYz0123456789
     You can't start until Finnbot has it: until then your answers wait, and you're switched on
     at the first hourly check after it's in (section 5).

THE COLUMNS (row 1)

  Column name          Required?  What to put in it
  -------------------  ---------  -------------------------------------------------------------
  symbol               Yes        The stock's symbol as Yahoo Finance writes it (see below).
  shares_qty           Yes        How many shares you own. Fractions are fine (0.75, 312.4567).
  avg_purchase_price   Yes        Your average price PER SHARE, in the currency the stock trades
                                  in (see below). 0 or blank means "unknown": the stock is still
                                  tracked, but its gain or loss shows as unknown.
  account_name         No         Which account it's in, e.g. TFSA, RRSP, ISA, Margin. Shown on
                                  the stock's card and used to total each account.
  currency             No         Leave blank (recommended): Finnbot detects it. If you fill it
                                  in, use a 3-letter code: USD, CAD, EUR, GBP, AUD, COP.

  Any other column (for example "notes") is ignored, so you can keep your own notes beside them.

SYMBOLS
  - US stocks and ETFs: the plain symbol: AAPL, MSFT, VOO.
  - Share classes use a hyphen, not a dot: BRK-B (not BRK.B).
  - Other exchanges add a suffix after a dot:
      Exchange                                Suffix(es)                  Currency
      Toronto (TSX, Venture, NEO, CSE)        .TO  .V  .NE  .CN           CAD
      London                                  .L                          GBP, priced in pence
      Australia                               .AX                         AUD
      Colombia                                .CL                         COP
      Germany (Xetra, Frankfurt)              .DE  .F                     EUR
      Paris, Amsterdam, Milan, Madrid         .PA  .AS  .MI  .MC          EUR
      Brussels, Lisbon, Vienna, Helsinki,     .BR  .LS  .VI  .HE  .IR     EUR
      Dublin
    Examples: SHOP.TO, RY.TO, SHEL.L, BHP.AX, SAP.DE, ASML.AS, CIBEST.CL.
  - Not sure? Search the company on finance.yahoo.com and copy the symbol it shows.
  - Exchanges not listed (for example Switzerland's .SW) may load, but their daily changes and
    currency handling aren't fully supported.

PRICES
  - Enter avg_purchase_price in the currency the stock trades in: US dollars for AAPL, Canadian
    dollars for SHOP.TO, euros for SAP.DE, pesos for CIBEST.CL.
  - London stocks (.L) are priced in PENCE. A share bought at £34.00 is 3400. Leave the currency
    column blank for London stocks: writing GBP there makes them 100 times too large.
  - Type plain numbers: 3400 or 185.50. No currency symbols ($, £), no thousands separators
    (72,000), no spaces. Leave the cells' format as Automatic or plain Number: Google may pass
    a formatted value like "72,000" to Finnbot as text, which would be read as unknown.

SPECIAL CASES
  - The same stock in several accounts: one row per account. Finnbot combines them on one card
    (total shares, combined gain or loss) and lists the accounts.
  - Several purchases in the same account: either one row with your average price, or one row per
    purchase. Both give the same totals.
  - The same company on two exchanges (for example SHOP and SHOP.TO): two separate rows and two
    separate cards, since they trade separately.
  - Sold a position completely? Delete its row (don't leave it with 0 shares).
  - Don't leave empty rows in the middle of the list.

EXAMPLE (row 1 is the column names)

  symbol     shares_qty  avg_purchase_price  account_name   currency
  AAPL       15          185.00              TFSA
  AAPL       8           362.00              Margin
  BRK-B      12          455.00              RRSP
  SHOP.TO    35          150.00              TFSA
  SHEL.L     120         3400                ISA
  SAP.DE     15          255.00              Brokerage
  BHP.AX     150         44.00               Brokerage
  CIBEST.CL  40          72000               Brokerage

2.3 Option C: Ideas only (no holdings)
--------------------------------------
What it is: you get the shared parts of the digest (market signals, macro news, swing and
long-term ideas) without connecting any holdings.
Pros: no setup beyond section 1, no API keys, no cost to you.
Cons: no portfolio overview, no reviews of your stocks, no holdings news, no Telegram alerts or
earnings reminders.
If you choose this, skip to section 4. A timezone (3.2) is optional; it only sets whether the
email says good morning, afternoon, or evening.
Every digest shows up to 3 long-term and 5 swing ideas (section 8). Want more or fewer? Ask Andres
for anywhere from 1 to 10 of each; this applies to every user, with holdings or not.

3. SETTINGS FOR USERS WITH HOLDINGS (OPTIONS A AND B)
================================================================================

3.1 Brokerage name
------------------
What it is: a label shown on your overview, for example "Questrade · 3 accounts · 12 positions".
Required: yes. For a Google Sheet, use whatever name you like ("My portfolio", your broker's name).

3.2 Timezone
------------
What it is: your local timezone, used to greet you by your time of day and to show when the
digest was prepared in your time as well as New York's.
Required: yes with holdings (optional for ideas only).
Format: a city-based timezone name, for example America/Toronto, America/New_York,
America/Bogota, Europe/London, Europe/Madrid, Australia/Perth, Australia/Sydney.

3.3 Home currency
-----------------
What it is: the currency you think in. Finnbot converts your whole portfolio into it at the top
of the email: the total value, the day's change, the unrealized gain or loss, each account's value,
and the amounts in the portfolio-risk notes.
Options: USD (default), CAD, EUR, GBP, AUD, COP.
What else you see: when it isn't USD, a line under the total shows the value in US dollars, the
exchange rate, and how your currency moved against the dollar this week, e.g.
"$108,503.66 USD · 1 GBP = 1.339 USD · GBP +1.0% this week".
Each holding's card stays in the stock's own currency (a Toronto stock in C$, a London stock in £
with its price in pence). The ideas are always in USD, since they're US stocks.
Cost: none.

3.4 The AI model that reviews your holdings
-------------------------------------------
What it is: at every digest, an AI model reviews each of your holdings: its price trend,
technical indicators, fundamentals, upcoming earnings, analyst views, and recent news. It then
gives the stock one of the five signals (section 0) with its reasoning. The watchdog (3.9) uses the
same model when it re-checks a stock.
Nothing to choose: you don't pick a model. Every user's holdings are reviewed by Claude Haiku 5.5,
from Anthropic, at low effort, and the same model writes your portfolio review.
Which key you need: an Anthropic key (3.6).
Cost: about $0.002 per stock, per review.
Why Haiku: in October 2026 Andres compared ten AI models and effort levels on the same holdings,
against the strongest available (Claude Opus 5.5). With Finnbot's review rules, Haiku gave the same
signal as Opus for about 9 holdings in 10, for about a fortieth of the price, and your holdings data
stays with Anthropic. No model's signals are a guarantee.

3.5 Effort level
----------------
What it is: how long the model "thinks" before answering.
Nothing to choose: low, for everyone, as tested in 3.4. It is already included in the cost in 3.4.

3.6 API keys (Anthropic, Gemini)
--------------------------------
What they are: an API key is a private code that lets Finnbot use an AI provider on your account,
so the provider bills you directly for your holdings' reviews. You need:

  Key        When you need it                          Used for
  ---------  ----------------------------------------  -------------------------------------------
  Anthropic  Always, with holdings                     Reviewing each holding, and the
                                                       portfolio review
  Gemini     With holdings and news on (3.7)           News for each holding (billing on)

Before you start: have a safe place ready to store each key the moment you create it (section 6).
Anthropic shows a key only once; if you lose it, you have to create a new one.

GETTING AN ANTHROPIC KEY (everyone with holdings)
  1. Go to platform.claude.com and sign in, or click to create an account (email or Google
     sign-in). This is Anthropic's developer console, separate from a claude.ai chat subscription:
     a Claude Pro or Max plan does NOT include API use.
  2. Add credit: open Billing (in Settings), add a payment card, and buy prepaid credit. For 10
     holdings, the Anthropic part is about $1 a month (3.10), so $5 lasts months.
  3. Recommended: in the console's limits settings, set a monthly spend limit, so a mistake or a
     leaked key can never cost more than you chose.
  4. Open Settings, then API keys, and click "Create key".
       - Name: Finnbot
       - Expiration: none, or a date you'll remember to renew (an expired key stops your reviews
         until you send a new one)
       - Linked account: yourself
  5. Copy the key (it starts with sk-ant-) and save it immediately in your password manager. The
     console never shows it again.

GETTING A GEMINI KEY (only if you want news for each holding, 3.7)
  1. Go to aistudio.google.com/api-keys and sign in with a Google account.
  2. Click "Create API key". Choose an existing Google Cloud project or let it create one (any name
     is fine, e.g. Finnbot).
  3. Turn on billing for that project: go to console.cloud.google.com, open Billing, and attach a
     payment card to the key's project. This is required: Google's free tier doesn't include the
     web search the news uses (tested on every current model in October 2026), so a key without
     billing gets no news at all.
  4. Recommended: in Billing, set a budget alert, so you're emailed if costs rise.
  5. Copy the key (it usually starts with AIza) and save it in your password manager.
  Cost: about $0.27 a month for 10 holdings and $0.53 for 20, with both digests (3.10). Each key
  also gets 5,000 free web searches a month, far more than Finnbot uses, so you pay only for the
  text the model writes.

Tips for both:
  - Create a key used ONLY for Finnbot. If it ever leaks, you can delete it without affecting
    anything else you use the provider for.
  - Check your usage page after the first week to confirm costs match section 3.10.
  - If a key stops working (expired, out of credit, billing off), the top of your email says so,
    and the rest of your digest still arrives. Create a new key, top up, or turn billing back on,
    and send Andres the new key if you made one.

3.7 News for each holding
-------------------------
What it is: for each holding, Gemini searches the web for its most important recent news and
writes up to 2 short summaries (medium priority or higher), each with a link to its source. They
appear on the stock's card and feed the portfolio review.
Options: on (default) or off.
Cost: about $0.001 per stock for a digest's news (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, chosen in October 2026:
about half the cost of the model before it, with news close to its quality). The Post-Market
digest shows the morning's news again for stocks that haven't moved much, kept their signal, and
haven't reported earnings since, so most of its news costs nothing.
How much you see: with up to 10 holdings, each card shows up to 2 stories. With more, each shows its
single most important story, except stocks flagged risky or urgent sell, which keep 2. This keeps
the email under Gmail's size limit.
Pros (on): explains WHY a stock is moving, with sources; the reviews can weigh real events.
Cons (on): needs a Gemini key with billing on; costs a little.
Turning it off means you don't need a Gemini key at all.

3.8 Telegram
------------
What it is: Telegram is a free messaging app. Finnbot's Telegram bot sends you:
  - Urgent sell alerts, at most once per stock per day: from each digest, as soon as it's sent,
    and with the watchdog (3.9) also between digests.
  - If you buy a stock from one of Finnbot's swing ideas (by the end of the next trading day), an
    alert when it reaches the idea's stop or target, and a monthly scorecard of how the ideas you
    bought did against the S&P 500. (Section 9 explains how Finnbot notices the purchase.)
  - Earnings reminders: about 24 hours before a stock you hold reports earnings, once per report,
    with Finnbot's latest call on it.
  - Notices when your holdings can't be read, for example the sheet isn't shared with Finnbot or
    your brokerage connection expired. (Problems with an API key show in the email instead.)
Only the sell alerts and the stop or target alerts are marked urgent.
Your three choices in the form:
  Yes, with the watchdog      Everything above. The watchdog (3.9) runs with its standard settings,
                              so an urgent sell can reach you at any time from 4:00 AM to 8:00 PM
                              ET on trading days, not only at digests. It also takes a closer look
                              around earnings: in the 4 trading days before a report and on the
                              trading day after it.
  Yes, without the watchdog   Everything above, but only as each digest finds it: urgent sells and
                              stop and target alerts arrive right after a digest instead of during
                              the day. Earnings reminders and notices are the same.
  No Telegram                 None of the above, and no watchdog or tripwire. Urgent sells and
                              holdings problems appear only in the email.
Required: no. Telegram is only for users with holdings.
Cost: free.

Steps:
  1. Install Telegram (phone or desktop) and create an account.
  2. Open Finnbot's bot (Andres will send you the link) and press Start. A bot can't message you
     until you've done this.
  3. Find your chat ID: send any message to @userinfobot in Telegram; it replies with your ID, a
     number like 123456789. (Or tell Andres once you've pressed Start, and he can find it.)
  4. Send Andres the number.

3.9 The watchdog: how often, when, and the tripwire
---------------------------------------------------
What it is: between digests, the watchdog re-checks your holdings during the day and sends an
urgent sell alert on Telegram if one is needed. It needs Telegram (3.8). Every digest already
reviews all your holdings and sends any urgent sell right after it arrives, so the watchdog
covers the hours between digests.
Your choice: only on or off ("Yes, with the watchdog" in the form). It runs with the standard
settings below: every 4 hours, extended hours, tripwire on. You can change any of them by asking
Andres; the options are listed under each, and section 5 lists everything else you can change.

How a check works, and why it stays cheap: each check first looks at prices without any AI.
Only stocks that need it get a new AI review:
  - the price moved about one average day's range since the stock's last review, or
  - it reports earnings within 4 trading days, or reported on the previous trading day (the day the
    market reacts to a report released after the close), or
  - its last review is more than 24 hours old.
Every other stock keeps its latest signal. There is also a hard cap of 40 AI reviews per day for
your watchdog, whatever happens.

A) How often (hours between checks)
   Standard: every 4 hours. Other options: 1 to 168 hours, or 0 (off).
     1 hour     Fastest reaction to news. More checks, so more reviews (still capped).
     4 hours    The standard: about 3-4 checks across a trading day's extended hours. The clock
                counts from the last check or digest, and a check that would land within about
                1.5 hours of a digest is skipped, since the digest reviews everything anyway.
                One more check runs shortly before the 4:00 PM close.
     24 hours   One check a day.

B) When it runs (session)
     Extended (standard) 4:00 AM-8:00 PM ET on trading days, including pre-market and
                        after-hours, when many companies release earnings. Outside regular
                        hours it uses the pre-market or after-hours price.
     Regular            9:30 AM-4:00 PM ET on trading days (shorter on half-days). Fewer checks,
                        so a little less cost, but it misses earnings news that lands before the
                        open or after the close until the next check.
     Around the clock   Every hour, every day, weekends and holidays included. Stocks barely
                        trade then, so this mostly adds checks without adding much.

C) The tripwire
   What it is: a quick price check every hour between scheduled checks. If a stock moves about
   twice its average daily range, it gets an AI review right away instead of waiting for the next
   scheduled check. It pauses for the hour after a digest, which has just reviewed everything.
   Standard: on. Other option: off.
   Cost: nothing unless a big move triggers a review.

Watchdog cost per month (21 trading days). The watchdog has only run on a schedule since late
October 2026, so the first two columns are still estimates; the last is the hard maximum.

6. KEEPING YOUR KEYS AND DATA SAFE
================================================================================

6.1 Why an API key matters
--------------------------
An API key is like a password and a credit card in one. Anyone who has your key can:
  - Anthropic and Gemini keys: run as many AI requests as they like, billed to YOU, until
    your credit or spend limit runs out. Leaked keys are found and abused within minutes; there are
    automated programs that search the internet for them all day.
  - SnapTrade Consumer Key: read all of your brokerage positions and balances, and possibly place
    trades where trading is enabled.
The providers can't tell a thief's requests from yours, and generally won't refund them.

6.2 Store every key safely the moment you create it
---------------------------------------------------
Anthropic and SnapTrade show a key only once. If you don't save it right away, you'll
have to create a new one.
  - Best: a password manager (for example Bitwarden, 1Password, or the one built into your
    iPhone, Android, or browser). Save each key as its own entry, e.g. "Finnbot - Anthropic key",
    with the date you created it.
  - Acceptable: a note on your own device that's locked with a passcode, or an encrypted file.
  - Not safe: a plain text file in a shared or synced folder, an email to yourself, a photo or
    screenshot, a sticky note, a shared document, or any chat message you send to yourself.
Keep your copy even after you've sent it to Andres: you'll need it to check usage, and you can
recognise which key is yours if you ever have to delete it.

6.3 Be very careful how keys travel online
------------------------------------------
Every place a key is pasted is a place it can leak from. Never put a key in:
  - The Finnbot sign-up form (it asks you NOT to; its answers are stored in a spreadsheet).
  - A group chat, a public post, a forum, a comment, or a support ticket.
  - An ordinary email or text message (they're stored in plain text on several servers and
    backed up indefinitely).
  - A screenshot or screen share (images of keys are read by the same automated programs).
  - A shared document or spreadsheet, or a code repository (for example GitHub).
  - An AI chatbot or assistant.
How to send your keys to Andres:
  - Only through the private channel Andres tells you to use, for example an end-to-end encrypted
    messenger such as Signal, or your password manager's secure-sharing feature.
  - Send them directly to Andres, one-to-one. Never to a group, even a private one.
  - Double-check the recipient before you send.
  - Delete the message on your side once Andres confirms he has them.
  - Never send a key to anyone who asks for it "on behalf of Finnbot": Andres will never ask
    you to send keys anywhere else, and nobody else is entitled to them.

6.4 Limit the damage in advance
-------------------------------
  - Create a key used only for Finnbot, so you can delete it without affecting anything else.
  - Set a monthly spend limit (Anthropic) or a budget alert (Google), so a leak can never cost
    much.
  - Turn on two-factor authentication for each provider account and for SnapTrade.
  - Look at each provider's usage page now and then. Usage you don't recognise is the first sign of
    a leak.

6.5 If you think a key has leaked
---------------------------------
  1. Delete (revoke) it immediately on the provider's website: the key stops working at once.
  2. Create a new key and save it safely (6.2).
  3. Send the new key to Andres privately (6.3) and tell him what happened.
  4. Check the provider's usage and billing pages for charges you don't recognise, and contact the
     provider's support if you find any.

6.6 Where your data goes
------------------------
  - Your holdings (symbols, share counts, prices paid) are sent to Anthropic, whose AI model
    reviews them and writes the portfolio review (with your account names). Gemini's news
    searches use only the symbols and company names.
  - With SnapTrade, SnapTrade can read your brokerage positions.
  - With Google Sheets, only you and Finnbot's service account can see it (2.2).
  - Andres sees your settings and keys in order to run Finnbot for you, and never shares them.

8. HOW FINNBOT FINDS ITS IDEAS
================================================================================

Both lists start every digest from the same place: every US-listed stock (NYSE, Nasdaq, NYSE
American) that is large (currently $8 billion in market value and up), trades at least 1 million
shares a day, and costs at least $15 a share. That is several hundred stocks. Two screens then
narrow them down, the survivors are scored by an AI model, and the best scores become the ideas.
The scoring model is DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, chosen in a comparison against stronger models; Andres pays
for it.
How many: up to 3 long-term and 5 swing ideas per digest; ask Andres for anywhere from 1 to 10 of
each. A day with fewer stocks above the pass mark shows fewer.
"Different industries" below uses Yahoo Finance's industries (about 140, such as Semiconductors
or Regional Banks), so two Technology stocks that do different things can both be picked. "Tech"
means the Technology and Communication Services sectors.

8.1 Long-term ideas: businesses worth owning for years
  1. Quality screen (Yahoo Finance): at least 8 analysts follow it and their average rating leans
     Buy (2.4 or better, on a scale where 1 is Strong Buy and 3 is Hold); expected to be profitable
     over the next year, at a forward P/E of 35 or less; a net
     profit margin of at least 7%; revenue up at least 5% on a year ago; a return on equity of at
     least 12%.
  2. Confirmation (Finnhub, over the last 12 months): revenue up at least 9%, earnings per share
     growing, debt at most 1.75 times equity, and a P/E between 0 and 65.
  3. Not overheated: a trend-strength reading (ADX) of 45 or less.
     About 100 stocks usually get this far; the 35 that rate best on the scoring's own measures
     go on to be scored.
  4. Scoring: the model scores each one out of 100 on valuation, financial health, growth, and
     competitive advantage (its "moat"). A score within 5 points of the pass mark is scored twice
     more and the middle score kept, so one unlucky run can't decide a close call.
  5. Picking: ideas scoring 85 or more, highest first, each from a different industry. At least a
     third of the list, rounded up, is outside tech (1 of 3), and those may score from 80. These
     mix rules only choose between stocks: if they'd leave the list short, it's filled with the
     best of the stocks they left out. On a day few stocks reach 85, the list is shorter.
Each card shows the score and its four parts, the model's thesis and key risk, the forward P/E,
PEG, revenue growth, profit margin, and two momentum readings: RSI (overbought at 70+, oversold at
30 or less) and the 12-month price return.

8.2 Swing ideas: trades over the next few weeks
  1. A looser quality screen: at least 3 analysts, a consensus of Hold or better, profitable now and
     expected to stay so (forward P/E up to 60), and revenue at most 5% below a year ago.
  2. A setup in the daily prices, one of two kinds:
       - A pullback: at least 1.5 normal days' ranges (ATR) below its recent high, but not more
         than 30% down, and at most 5% below its 200-day average, so the longer trend is intact.
       - A new uptrend: its 9-day average crossed above its 21-day average in the last 5 sessions.
     Either way: at least $10 a share and $20 million traded a day, an RSI between 20 and 65, not
     still falling, and no earnings report within 5 trading days.
  3. A trade plan worked out by Finnbot itself, the same way for every stock: the stop just under
     the nearest support (and at least 1.5 normal days' ranges below the entry), the target at the
     nearest resistance above it (a recent high, a moving average, or the upper Bollinger Band),
     1.5 to 8 ranges up. A plan with less than 1.5
     times as much to gain as to lose is dropped. The best 40 plans go on to scoring.
  4. Scoring: the model scores out of 100 on the technical setup, the plan's reward against its
     risk (worked out by Finnbot, not the model), momentum and volume, and catalysts and sentiment.
     Close calls are scored again, as above.
  5. Picking: ideas scoring 75 or more that are still early in their move (not already run up from
     their recent low, RSI 70 or less, not above the upper Bollinger Band), with more than half of
     them from different industries, and at least 1 outside tech in a list of 4 or more (2 in a
     list of 8 or more). As above, a list the mix rules would leave short is filled with the best
     of the rest.
Each card shows the plan (entry, stop, target, and the ratio of gain to risk), what the stop and
target are based on, and the technical readings behind the setup.

Both lists are US stocks, priced in US dollars. They are AI-scored ideas from public data, not
advice: always do your own research before buying.

9. HOW FINNBOT ASSESSES YOUR HOLDINGS
================================================================================

At every digest (and at each watchdog check, 3.9), each of your holdings gets one of the five
signals (section 0) and a short reason, from Claude Haiku 5.5 on your Anthropic key.

9.1 What the model sees, per holding
  - The price path: the last 21 daily prices and volumes, and technical indicators (RSI, 9- and
    21-day averages, Bollinger Bands, Parabolic SAR, beta).
  - The business: fundamentals, analysts' ratings and targets, insider buying and selling, recent
    headlines, the next earnings date, and past earnings surprises.
  - Your position: shares, what you paid, the gain or loss, and its share of your portfolio.
  - If you bought it from a Finnbot swing idea: the idea's plan (entry, stop, target).

9.2 Facts worked out first, by Finnbot itself
So that every review reads the chart the same way, Finnbot computes these before the model sees
the stock, and the model applies them as given:
  - The long-term trend: intact (above its 200-day average, with the 50-day above the 200-day),
    mixed, or weak.
  - A pullback (at least 1.5 normal days' ranges below its 20-day high), or a recent run (up 15% or
    more, or 4 ranges, from its 20-day low).
  - Signs the selling is ending (0 to 4): RSI rising, heavier volume on up days, a higher low, and a
    close above its 9-day average (or its stop-and-reverse level below the price).
  - Stretched (RSI 70+ or above the upper Bollinger Band), trading days to the next earnings report,
    and whether it's a fund or a leveraged ETF.

9.3 The rules, checked in this order (the first that applies wins)
  1. Urgent sell: a specific, recent event that can't wait for the next digest, such as fraud,
     bankruptcy risk, a guidance collapse, or a failed key product; or a sharp breakdown through
     support on heavy volume. Never for slow drift, valuation alone, or a normal-sized move.
  2. Profit sell: a real recent run plus at least two signs it's tiring (stretched, RSI falling
     from a high, at the analysts' target or a prior high, momentum fading, or valuation stretched).
  3. Risky: one serious warning (missed earnings twice or cut guidance, two analyst downgrades,
     news that damages the reason to own it, or a broken trend that's still falling), or two
     lesser ones (a short-term downtrend with no sign of a turn, earnings within about 5 trading
     days, one downgrade, heavy insider selling, shrinking margins, an unexplained move twice its
     normal size). An upcoming earnings report alone is not enough.
  4. Buy more: a pullback in an intact or mixed long-term trend, with at least 2 of the 4 signs the
     selling is ending, not stretched, no warning from rule 3, and the business unchanged.
  5. Hold: everything else.
Funds and ETFs (leveraged ones included) are judged on their price trend alone, since they have no
earnings or analysts of their own.
Note: buy more means "it dipped, and now is a good time to add". A stock you just bought from a
swing idea that started a new uptrend usually shows hold: keep it, and follow the idea's plan.

9.4 Second looks
An urgent sell or profit sell is reviewed twice more, and the majority wins (the milder signal on a
tie), so a one-off reading can't send you an alert. The model also rates its overall outlook on
the stock (bullish, neutral, or bearish), separately from the signal.

9.5 The rest of the holdings section
  - News (3.7): up to 2 stories per holding from Gemini, with links, shown on its card.
  - The portfolio review: up to 5 notes on the portfolio as a whole (concentration in a stock,
    sector, or currency; stocks likely to move together; overall market sensitivity; earnings
    dates close together; how much is flagged risky), written by Claude Haiku from the figures in
    your digest.
  - Reuse: a review the watchdog made in the last hour, for a stock that hasn't moved since, is
    reused instead of asking the model again.
  - Bought from an idea: Finnbot notices a new position in a stock it suggested the day before (by
    the end of the next trading day), labels its card with the idea, gives the model the idea's
    plan, and sends the alerts in 3.8.
